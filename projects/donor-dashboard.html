A donor dashboard that shows a crime victim nonprofit which donors come back

I analyzed Crime Victim Center (CVC) donor records, found that a small group of donors gave most of the dollars, and built a five-page Streamlit dashboard that repeats that check, plus retention, map and campaign views, on each new GiveButter export. It fully covers the brief's donor-ranking goal and partly covers segmentation, tracking and outreach. A 2026 re-test found four silent bugs: dashboards need tests on known data.

MotivationWhy it mattered

Objective: CVC's brief asked for a donor strategy aimed at high-value and monthly donors. Five of its goals need donor data: find high-value donors, segment them, track them on GiveButter, plan a recurring-gift campaign, and guide outreach.

Why it matters: CVC, a St. Louis nonprofit, served 6,624 victims and survivors of crime and violence in 2023, yet donations and fundraisers were only about 8% of its 2022 revenue. Keeping the right donors counts.

What I learned

Dashboards fail quietly. Re-running the app on synthetic data in 2026 turned up four bugs that raise no error, such as a +51,062.7% growth label and a map left empty by one blank ZIP cell. Now I would ship a test file with checks on counts, ZIP format and axis types (see Methods).

First-pass analysis (March 2025)

Before building the app I worked in a Python notebook. I charted per-campaign totals for 2022, 2023 and 2024 side by side in matplotlib and highlighted the three largest campaigns across all years. Those totals were typed in from the Fundraisers tabs of CVC's 2022 and 2023 spreadsheets and a MATLAB summary of the 2024 GiveButter export. The Pareto and organization breakdowns ran in pandas on a combined 2022 to 2024 donor sheet: group gifts by donor, sort by total, compute each donor's running share of all dollars, and keep donors up to 80%. Seaborn drew the chart. The analysis showed that a small group of donors gave most of the dollars, and that a few signature events brought in most of the event revenue.

Cleaning and merging (Figure 5)

The export layout the app was built for has a title line above the column names, so it reads row 2 as the header. It strips stray spaces from column names and renames duplicates (a second “Email” becomes “Email_2”) so pandas can select them, then renames three GiveButter columns to Date, Donation Amount and ZIP; the pages also read Email and Campaign Title as they are. Rows with neither a first name nor an organization are dropped. Amounts and dates are converted with errors='coerce', so a bad cell becomes a blank instead of crashing the app, and a file that fails to parse shows a warning. Each row is tagged Individual or Organization (by whether an organization name is present) and with its source file. The app tracks file names, so uploading a file twice does not double-count it, and removing a file removes its rows as long as another file stays loaded. Nothing is saved between sessions, and CVC's 2022 and 2023 spreadsheets use another layout.

Retention, churn and cohorts

  • Donor key: email address, used for unique and repeat donor counts, retention, cohorts and the top-donor ranking.
  • Returning vs. one-time: a donor with more than one gift is returning; the app labels single-gift donors “New”.
  • Quarterly churn: a donor-by-quarter activity grid (1 if they gave). A donor active in one quarter but not the next counts as churned; churn rate is churned divided by active. Someone who gives once a year counts as churned after every gift, so quarterly churn runs high by construction. The cohort view looks further ahead.
  • Cohorts (Figure 4): a donor's cohort is the quarter of their first gift. Every gift gets an offset, quarters since that first gift, computed on one global quarter index so offsets stay consistent across years. Each cell is the number of the cohort's donors who gave at that offset divided by the cohort's size (blue map), or their total dollars (a second tab, green map). Offsets where no one gave stay blank rather than zero. Comparing cohorts down one column is a like-for-like comparison.
  • Recurring gifts: the app does not read GiveButter's recurring-plan (Frequency) field.

Map and campaigns (Figure 6 and the Fundraising page)

Spreadsheets drop leading zeros, so ZIP codes are padded back to five digits before a pgeocode (GeoNames postal database) lookup. The bar chart shows the top 20 ZIPs by total dollars (the chart library hides alternate labels when they crowd). On the Fundraising page, staff pick any campaign to see its year-over-year totals. The app strips a trailing year from each title, so a demo title like “Spring Appeal 2021” (Figure 5) merges with the same appeal in other years, and it fills a full campaign-by-year grid, so a year before a campaign launched shows as $0 instead of disappearing. The page also compares total raised and average gift per campaign (the Home page's campaign table adds gift counts), and plots gifts up to $1,000 with an adjustable bin width.

What the 2026 re-test found

  • Bugs: the churn chart treats quarter labels as dates, so its x-axis shows no quarters; the “Organizations” tile counts gifts, not organizations (177 on demo data with 100); before a date range is picked, the growth label divides by the first month's small total (+51,062.7%); and when ZIPs are stored as numbers, one blank cell makes pandas read the column as decimals (“63130.0”), so the map comes out empty.
  • Fragile input: the app always reads row 2 as the header, to match the file CVC shared, so an export saved with headers in row 1 loads zero rows.
  • Consequence: staff would see a wrong number, or an empty chart, with no warning.
  • Check I'd add: a small synthetic test file shipped with the app, assertions on unique counts, ZIP format and axis types, and header-row detection on upload.
  • Decide what a “donor” is before counting donors. The app keys donors by email address: a gift with no email drops out of the retention counts, and one person with two addresses counts twice. I would now make the donor key an explicit, documented choice and show staff how many gifts it leaves out.

Demo data for this page

To protect donor privacy, the screenshots use a seeded synthetic dataset in a simplified version of the GiveButter export layout (a title row, then 12 of the export's 64 columns, including the seven the app reads): 1,200 fictional donors (1,100 individuals, 100 organizations) and 2,613 gift rows from 2021 to 2024, with real public ZIP codes, mostly in the St. Louis area. The demo is larger than CVC's real records and starts a year earlier. It includes 24 anonymous rows and padded column names so the cleaning step has real work to do; after cleaning, 2,589 rows remain. The Home screenshot uses the app's real uploader; the other pages load the same files through a small demo-only helper that runs Home.py's own upload code.
